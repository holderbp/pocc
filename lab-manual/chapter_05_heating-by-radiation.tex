\section{Experiments and Activities}


\subsection{Specific heat of aluminum}


energy flow diagram for system at two different time intervals, prior to aluminum cylinder, and at the time of the drop (when aluminum is heating up)
--> decide how hot you want the water to begin with


take apart calorimetry device and explain each piece

experiment to watch the temperature decrease in and out of the cup? How well does calorimetry device do?

measure mass of aluminum cylinder

put aluminum cylinder in beaker with ice water, check  to see it is at zero C

get hot (~50C water from faucet) put a few cm in calorimetry cup (you will measure its volume afterward)

Put calorimetry cup in device

start the stopwatch

monitor temperature for at least 5 min with readings every 30 s. Lift thermometer above base, use the stirrer

drop in Al cylinder using scissors as tongs, record time of drop

continue to monitor temperature in 30s intervals for at least 5 min.

make plot of temperature vs time.  For times before and after drop, make best-fit linear lines

at the time of the drop measure the vertical distance (temp) between these lines, that is deltaT for the water.

also record the lower temperature (lower temperature straight line) of the water at that time, that is Tf

pour out water from calorimetry cup into 100mL grad cylinder (count number of full 100mL volumes and add to final amount under 100mL)

calculate specific heat of aluminum, using 4.2 J/g/K for water


\subsection{ Heating aluminum block by radiation}

Put lamp 5 cm away from block and with its rays at right angle to block

put bit of aluminum foil in the hole to make entry of thermometer stiff

place in styrofoam holder and stick thermometer through

start stopwatch before turning on lamp

record temperature every minute or so, for about 20 minutes 

(you can record with the go-pro, but the post-analysis, watching the video to get temps, might be just as annoying as taking the data; 
alternatively, you could have a phone take photos every 30s, 
or use time-lapse (of both stopwatch and thermometer), or set a lower resolution video)

calculate the expected energy flux from the aluminum at that temperature... how might that affect results?
